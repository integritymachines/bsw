# Forensic inquiry into possible BSW second-vote losses in the 2025 Bundestag election

## 1. Scope

In the 2025 federal election, newcomer party BSW received 2,472,947 of 49,649,512 valid second votes, or 4.981%, in the certified result. At a fixed denominator, another 9,529 votes would have brought the party to the 5% threshold required for parliamentary representation. This forensic inquiry asks whether administrative errors systematically reduced BSW’s preliminary count, whether some of those errors survived certification, and whether their residual effect could have changed the electoral outcome. It concerns the counting, recording, and reporting of ballots as marked by voters. Voting errors are not considered. [Certified national result][nat25]

## 2. Headline findings

Three nested hypotheses are tested. The causal claim is that a directional administrative error materially disadvantaged BSW in the original, preliminary count, that the error persisted after certification, and that the residual error after certification is material enough to put the expected vote share after a full recount sufficiently above the published vote share to warrant further inquiry. Conversely, the null hypotheses are: 1. (Occurrence) No directional error occurred during the original count. 2. (Persistence) All instances of directional error were accounted for and corrected with the official count. 3. (Materiality) Any residual directional error remaining after the official count is too small to warrant further inquiry. All null hypotheses need to be refuted to establish the full claim. The public evidence does not fully refute the third.

Additionally, less intrusive remedies than a full recount are considered. No legal or political assessment is offered. No final conclusions are proposed. This report does not attempt to replace a recount but reduce the uncertainty of such an undertaking. The forensic inquiry fully focuses on whether a potential material administrative error can be reconstructed from the publicly accessible data.

1. **Occurrence.** Reject the null that BSW’s preliminary-to-final gain of 4,277 votes was an ordinary certification adjustment for a party of its size under either established-party baseline tested. The one-sided predictive probability was 0.0025 using the preceding two elections and 0.0015 using three. Adding the 2025 corrections of the two established parties of comparable size gives 0.0001 and 0.0002. Ballot position, the unequal numbers of BSW and Bündnis Deutschland (BD) votes exposed to confusion, and verified transfers supply a clerical explanation with a predicted net direction adverse to BSW. [2025 corrections][corr25] · [Ballot order][order25] · [Documented corrections][bt3100] · Historical sources
2. **Persistence.** Reject, on the balance of the forensic evidence, the null that consolidation removed the entire directional component. Across 57,812 eligible precincts, 10 certified returns combined an extreme BSW deficit with an extreme BD surplus under both geographic reference specifications. The corresponding count was zero for FDP–BD and zero when FDP was allowed its strongest selected minor-party comparator. Local political variation does not reproduce the signature. In the flagged precincts, BD’s second votes are not matched by its first votes. Comparably sized parties in 2017, 2021, and 2025 show neither BSW’s excess of extreme low counts nor its destination-matched deficits. [Precinct returns][wbz25] · [2017 precinct returns][wbz17] · [2021 precinct returns][wbz21]
3. **Materiality.** On the balance of the evidence, reject the starting null that the expected BSW share after a full recount equals its certified share. The expected correction is upward. A separate question is whether it is large enough to reach 5%. The conspicuous-only scenario of approximately +250 votes is excluded by the persistence finding: the most conspicuous errors survived certification, so less conspicuous errors survived at least at that rate. The full-susceptibility scenario of approximately +28,500 votes has the supported mechanism but overstates its degree. The national residual lies below it by a penalty on the susceptible fraction, on the mean net loss, or on both. The certified returns fix only their product. The expected correction reaches the 5% threshold unless the surviving diffuse rate lies more than about 70% below the yield found in the screened recounts. The intermediate scenario, approximately +10,060 to +10,510 with the estimated visible allowance, marks that point. Where a possible correction warrants further inquiry is for the deciding body to judge. Neither the null that BSW would remain below 5% nor the causal alternative that surviving errors put it over 5% can be refuted from the public evidence. The headline probability model assigns a 70% to 72% chance of reaching 5%, conditional on its assumptions about the diffuse residual. The public evidence does not settle either the third null or the actual recount outcome.
4. **Verification.** The first step is publication of precinct-level preliminary second-vote counts with identifiers linking them to the certified returns. This would reveal how much of the +4,277 recovery came from large corrections, how much came from small signed changes, and which categories moved together. If the electoral question remains unresolved, a targeted recount of conspicuous survivors and a separate random probe of the remaining precincts would measure the two outstanding components.

## 4. Limitations

The public evidence examined here cannot supply a complete precinct-level chain from preliminary return through certification or a representative post-certification examination of ballots. National party totals identify how much each category gained or lost. They do not identify the underlying gross flows. A gain of 20 BSW votes and a loss of 20 BD votes are consistent with a transfer between them, but several offsetting changes can produce the same margins. [2025 corrections][corr25] · [Bundestag 21/3100][bt3100]

The 50 screened recounts occurred during certification. Their net gain is already included in the certified result. Their value is that they reveal information about the probable sign and size of errors found outside specified BSW anomaly screens. Their selection and timing limit what their average can establish about the residual after certification. [Petition][petition] · [Bundestag 21/3100][bt3100]

Visibility depends on the receiving category. A misattribution stands out in certified returns only where the receiving party’s count is small. Transfers into a large party leave no detectable signature. Estimates built on visible cases are therefore lower bounds.

The certified 2021 precinct returns incorporate the 2024 Berlin repeat election. Berlin is excluded from the 2021 comparisons. Outside Berlin, the file matches the original certification. [2021 precinct returns][wbz21]

## 5. Structure of the forensic inquiry

A normal causal inquiry starts with a public and well-documented intervention and tries to assess the downstream effect (direction, significance, magnitude) such an intervention would cause, against alternative explanations.

This forensic inquiry starts with accessible downstream data of varying granularity, and an unobserved hypothesized directional intervention as cause, and asks if the downstream data is commensurate with such an intervention against other explanations, including purely random background error.

For most precincts we know the reported outcome but cannot observe which ballots were misallocated (if any), which errors prompted verification, or which survived. Selecting low-BSW precincts and then treating low BSW support as proof of negligence would assume the cause under investigation rather than establish it.

The inquiry therefore derives observable consequences of the proposed clerical mechanism and tests each against a rival explanation at the appropriate level. Primary evidence is statistical evidence bearing directly on the hypothesis at that level. Subsidiary evidence is provided as needed. For Occurrence, the national uplift between preliminary and certified results is considered. The primary test is the historical distribution of national party corrections. For Persistence, it is the distribution of joint anomalies across the certified precinct population. For Materiality, it is the ability of national residual models to reproduce the observed distribution. Individual cases identify mechanisms and illustrate if and how errors might have escaped detection. They do not estimate prevalence.

The inquiry does not propose perfect counting as the null model, just undirected random error of a credible magnitude, which would put the expected BSW vote share near the official vote share, and would make a realized uplift of near +10,000 votes for BSW an event of vanishingly small likelihood.

## 6. Derivation of the causal hypotheses

As a newcomer, BSW appeared below incumbent parties ordered by their previous Land results at or near the bottom. Additionally, minor party BD appeared immediately above BSW in 15 of 16 Länder. (Saarland placed the Greens between them.) Both full names began with Bündnis, raising the possibility of confusion in the recording or reporting of tallies. These observations suggest two mechanisms with different predictions. A general down-ballot penalty could cause BSW votes to be overlooked or recorded in several other categories. Confusion between the two similarly-named, adjacent entries specifically predicts increased misattribution of BSW and BD votes on the individual vote and the stack level, leading to a net BSW deficit accompanied by a net BD surplus. [Ordering rule][orderrule] · [Official ballot order][order25]

The predicted direction follows from the difference in vote mass. Let $B_i$, $D_i$ denote true BSW and BD votes in precinct $i$. Let $X_i$ count BSW ballots recorded as BD and $Y_i$ count BD ballots recorded as BSW. Within this channel,

$$B_i^{\mathrm{obs}} = B_i - X_i + Y_i, \qquad D_i^{\mathrm{obs}} = D_i + X_i - Y_i.$$

If $a_i$ and $b_i$ are the respective per-ballot confusion probabilities, then

$$E\left[X_i - Y_i \mid B_i, D_i\right] = a_i B_i - b_i D_i.$$

Bidirectional confusion can generate a systematic net loss. At equal per-ballot error rates, expected gross flows are proportional to the number of ballots exposed. BSW had about 32 times as many votes nationally as BD. A common confusion rate would therefore generate about 32 BSW→BD errors per reverse error. The hypothesis requires neither one-way mistakes nor conscious preference by counting staff. [Certified national result][nat25]

The unit of error determines conspicuousness. If a true count of 24 BSW and 1 BD is reported as 23 and 2, the net loss is one. One forward and zero reverse errors, or two forward and one reverse error, would both produce it. If the two stacks or subtotals are exchanged, the same true count becomes 1 and 24: 25 ballots assigned to the wrong party, 23 net BSW votes lost, under one clerical error. Both errors preserve the combined total. The latter creates a strong local anomaly. The former can leave an entirely ordinary-looking return undetectable from the outside with purely statistical means.

This distinction enters twice. Suspicion-driven certification is more likely to discover a conspicuous displacement. A statistical screen applied to certified returns is also more likely to discover conspicuous survivors. The visible stack cases can therefore help establish persistence without determining how many individual errors remain.

## 7. Formal model and null hypotheses

Let $P_i$ be preliminary BSW votes, $F_i$ certified BSW votes, and $T_i$ the count an accurate ballot examination would yield. The accounting identity is

$$\underbrace{T_i - P_i}_{\text{initial loss}} = \underbrace{F_i - P_i}_{\text{certification recovery}} + \underbrace{T_i - F_i}_{\text{residual}}.$$

National certification recovery was +4,277, producing 2,472,947 official votes cast for BSW. Initial and residual losses remain unobserved. The model considers a majority of diligent precincts with mean-zero signed error and a susceptible population with positive mean BSW loss. A susceptible precinct can still record every ballot correctly, a diligent precinct can still commit undirected clerical errors. The boundary between the two hypothesized types is by default unobservable. The national loss includes all administrative channels and all reverse errors. [2025 corrections][corr25]

| Proposition | Null | Causal alternative |
|---|---|---|
| I. Generation | Ordinary administrative error produced no systematic net BSW loss in the preliminary count. | An administrative process produced a positive net loss in a susceptible population. |
| II. Persistence | Certification removed the directional component. Local political variation and ordinary noise explain the remaining extremes. | Some directional errors survived and produced an excess of the predicted joint signature. |
| III. Materiality | The surviving net loss is too small to warrant further inquiry. | The surviving net loss is of sufficient magnitude to warrant further inquiry under the deciding body’s standard. |
| Electoral consequence | An accurate recount uncovers additional votes but leaves BSW below 5%. | Surviving net administrative loss is sufficient to bring BSW to at least 5%. |

The third null and the electoral null answer different questions. An expected upward correction of a few hundred votes is sufficient to reject equal expected shares on the forensic assessment. It would still leave BSW thousands of votes below 5%. The fundamental determination what constitutes a material uplift of electoral consequence is a matter of institutional deliberation and left open here.

## 8. Step-by-step investigation

### I. Occurrence: Was the +4,277 national uplift an ordinary certification adjustment?

**Null hypothesis.** The preliminary count contained ordinary clerical errors with no systematic net effect on BSW. Given the party’s vote total, its preliminary-to-final correction is drawn from the historical distribution of certification adjustments for comparably sized established parties.

**Causal alternative.** BSW’s low ballot position and proximity to the similarly named BD exposed it to counting and reporting errors. Confusion could operate in both directions. Because substantially more ballots were cast for BSW than for BD, comparable per-ballot error rates would produce a net BSW loss. Correction of part of that loss predicts an unusually large positive national certification adjustment relative to the historical baseline. Recovery from BD is a further prediction specific to the confusion mechanism.

**Primary test.** For each party-election observation, calculate the signed correction rate $r_p = (F_p - P_p)/P_p$. BSW gained 4,277 on 2,468,670 preliminary votes, or 17.3 per 10,000. Compare this rate with national corrections of established parties polling 3–8%. Using the national party correction as the observation avoids treating a 30-ballot transcription mistake as 30 statistically independent events. [2025 corrections][corr25] · [Certified national result][nat25]

Under a centered Gaussian background with unknown scale, divide the BSW rate by the root mean square of the $m$ historical rates. Under independent observations, the resulting statistic has a $t_m$ reference distribution. The two-election window uses 2017 and 2021. The three-election window adds 2013. Each window is also run with the 2025 corrections of FDP and CSU, the two established parties polling 3–8% in 2025. They share the election, the procedure, and the timetable with BSW. The 2021 comparison uses the original certification before the Berlin repeat election. [2013][wr13] · [2017 preliminary][wr17] · [2017 certified][nat17] · [2021 preliminary][heft2_21] · [2021 tables][kas21] · [2021 certified][nat21] · [2025 corrections][corr25]

| Historical calibration | Observations | Background scale per 10,000 | BSW uplift relative to scale | One-sided predictive probability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Established parties, 2017 and 2021 | 3 | 2.3 | 7.45 times | 0.0025 |
| Established parties, 2013, 2017, and 2021 | 5 | 3.2 | 5.40 times | 0.0015 |
| Three elections, including newcomer AfD 2013 as ordinary background | 6 | 9.6 | 1.80 times | 0.061 |
| Established parties, 2017, 2021, and 2025 | 5 | 1.87 | 9.25 times | 0.0001 |
| Established parties, 2013 to 2025 | 7 | 2.75 | 6.31 times | 0.0002 |
| Four elections, including newcomer AfD 2013 as ordinary background | 8 | 8.35 | 2.07 times | 0.036 |

**Finding.** Reject the ordinary established-party correction null under both historical windows. Adding an election leaves the result materially unchanged. Adding the same-election 2025 observations strengthens it. Classifying the historical newcomer as ordinary background changes it materially. With 2025 included, the result also holds at the 5% level under that classification. The distinction matters because the down-ballot hypothesis predicts exposure for that newcomer as well. With three to seven established-party observations, the numerical probabilities depend on the assumed background distribution.

**Subsidiary findings.**

- **Historical analogue.** AfD gained 4,613 votes in 2013, its first federal election, or 22.5 per 10,000 preliminary votes. In 2017 it gained 1,021, or 1.7 per 10,000. The larger newcomer correction supports the predicted direction of a down-ballot penalty. One party observed twice cannot isolate ballot position from other election-specific causes. [2013][wr13] · [2017 preliminary][wr17] · [2017 certified][nat17]
- **Geography and exposure.** BSW gained in all 16 federal states. Its proportional correction exceeded the FDP control in 15. Saarland was both the sole exception to that comparison and the sole state without BD–BSW adjacency. With only one nonadjacent state, this is corroboration of the predicted relationship. [2025 corrections][corr25] · [Official ballot order][order25]
- **Destination and verification.** BD lost 2,640 votes during certification. Because BD’s inflow scales with BSW’s vote mass, the absolute change is the comparable measure. BD’s loss is eight times the next-largest small-party loss (MLPD −325) and equals 62% of BSW’s gain. Ballot examinations documented actual transfers back to BSW, including a case where the rapid report and signed protocol had agreed. The aggregate signs match the predicted flow. The examinations establish that the pathway operated. [2025 corrections][corr25] · [Bundestag 21/3100][bt3100]
- **Down-ballot exposure.** Other small parties low on the ballot show large relative corrections: MLPD −1.6%, PdH −1.1%, MERA25 −1.9%, WerteUnion −1.0%. The established parties at the top of the ballot moved by less than 0.03%. The gap is the higher error rate low on the ballot that the general down-ballot penalty predicts. [2025 corrections][corr25] · [Official ballot order][order25]

### II. Persistence: Did certification eliminate the directional component?

**Null hypothesis.** Certification removed the directional BSW undercount. Local political variation and undirected recording noise account for the joint distribution of certified BSW and BD votes.

**Causal alternative.** Some net BSW→BD transfers survived certification. A surviving transfer of $k$ votes lowers the BSW count by $k$ and raises the BD count by $k$ in the same precinct. The predicted signature is therefore a local BSW deficit accompanied by a local BD surplus. A low BSW count alone is insufficient.

**Primary test.** Examine all 57,812 in-person precincts with at least 200 valid second votes. Under the statistical reference model, the target precinct and its local peers share the same underlying party probabilities. Conditional on the total votes for each party in target plus peers, its target count follows a hypergeometric distribution. For observed BSW and BD counts $b_i$ and $d_i$, calculate

$$p_i = \max\left\{\Pr_0\left(X_{i,\mathrm{BSW}} \le b_i\right), \Pr_0\left(X_{i,\mathrm{BD}} \ge d_i\right)\right\}.$$

Taking the larger probability requires both departures to be extreme and allows dependence between the two party counts. Apply Holm’s correction across all 57,812 precincts at a familywise significance level of 0.05. Run the test twice, excluding the target from its own baseline: first against constituency peers, then against municipality peers within the constituency where at least five peers and 1,000 peer votes are available, with constituency fallback elsewhere. Repeat the procedure for FDP against BD and for FDP against its strongest of five minor-party comparators, selected in held-out constituencies and adjusted for the five choices.

| Joint signature | Constituency reference | Municipality or fallback | Rejected under both |
|---|---|---|---|
| BSW deficit and BD surplus | 10 | 11 | 10 |
| FDP deficit and BD surplus | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| FDP deficit and strongest comparator surplus | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**Result.** Reject the global local-voting null under either reference. The smallest Holm-adjusted $p$-value was $1.6 \times 10^{-10}$ under each. The same precinct supplied both minima, so these are overlapping robustness checks. All ten precincts passing both tests reported zero BSW votes and between 12 and 33 BD votes, totaling 201. A literal reversal in all ten would recover 201 votes. [Certified precinct returns][wbz25]

**Countertest.** Among the 57,812 eligible precincts, 741 recorded three or fewer BSW votes, against 213 predicted by the local binomial model. For FDP the corresponding counts were 583 observed and 240 predicted. Both parties therefore show more low-count precincts than the model predicts. Under a less stringent test of joint depletion and approximate vote conservation, 21 BSW–BD cases passed both references, compared with 17 when FDP could use its strongest comparator. The BSW-specific evidence is consequently strongest at the extreme end. The local binomial model is therefore not a usable reference for local political variation. The tests below use the same voters’ first votes, ballot order, and comparator parties instead.

**Same voters, two columns.** Local political strength appears in both votes cast by the same voters. A counting or recording error in the second-vote column leaves the first-vote column untouched. A joint screen flagged precincts with at least 100 valid votes where both the BSW deficit and the BD surplus had one-sided binomial probabilities of at most 0.001 against constituency peers. In constituencies where BD fielded a candidate, 138 unflagged precincts recorded at least eight BD second votes. In none did BD’s first votes fall below half its second votes. Of the 13 flagged precincts in those constituencies, 11 did. Even at the 95% upper bound for the baseline rate, the probability of 11 or more is about $4 \times 10^{-17}$. Where BSW fielded a candidate, a BSW second-vote count below half its first-vote count occurs in 0.06% of precincts with at least five BSW first votes. [Precinct returns][wbz25]

**Ballot adjacency.** Voters’ geography does not depend on ballot order. Counting and recording errors do. For every ordered party pair in every Land, a swap signature is defined against constituency peers in precincts with at least 100 valid votes: the locally larger party, predicted at eight votes or more and at more than twice the smaller party’s prediction, falls to a quarter of its prediction or less, and the smaller party exceeds its own prediction by at least three quarters of the larger party’s. Per 10,000 eligible precinct pairs, the signature occurred 2.2 times for BSW→BD, 0.28 times for other ballot-adjacent pairs, and 0.12 times for non-adjacent pairs. Adjacency raises the rate for other pairs as well, as the general down-ballot penalty predicts. BSW→BD exceeds the other adjacent pairs eightfold. [Precinct returns][wbz25] · [Official ballot order][order25]

**Matched magnitudes.** A transfer moves the same ballots out of one category and into another. Political variation couples deficits and surpluses loosely. Among the 39 joint extremes of the screen with at least eight predicted BSW votes, the BD surplus and the BSW deficit correlate at 0.72, with a slope through the origin of 0.81. Politically related pairs correlate at 0.22 to 0.40 (Greens→AfD, CDU→Die Linke, AfD→Greens, each over 6,000 precincts), and BSW→Volt at 0.31. The comparison extends across elections. Where a minor party exceeded a mid-sized party that fell to half its predicted count or less, the BD surplus amounted to 0.64 of the BSW deficit. For every other pair examined in 2017, 2021, and 2025, adjacent or not, the minor-party surplus amounted to 0.22 to 0.39 of the deficit. Where those comparators fielded constituency candidates, their first votes fell with their second votes, to a median of 0.23 to 0.51 of prediction, as local political weakness predicts. BSW fielded candidates in three of its 20 such precincts, too few for the same check. [Precinct returns][wbz25] · [2017 precinct returns][wbz17] · [2021 precinct returns][wbz21]

**Historical countertest.** Local political variation is not specific to BSW. If it explains the extreme low counts, comparably sized parties should show them at a comparable rate. The countertest was repeated on the certified precinct returns of 2017 and 2021 for every nationwide party polling 4–10%, with FDP 2017 added as a control and the 2025 FDP and Die Linke series retained. CSU, which stands only in Bavaria, is excluded. Eligibility and the municipality reference with constituency fallback are unchanged. Berlin 2021 is excluded (Section 4). Precincts are compared at equal predicted counts so that party size and geography do not drive the comparison.

| Three or fewer votes, per 1,000 eligible precincts | Predicted 10–15 | 15–20 | 20–25 | 25 or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BSW 2025 | 22.8 | 2.5 | 1.4 | 0.25 |
| Six comparators | 9.4–28.7 | 1.7–8.7 | 0–0.86 | 0–0.07 |

Below a predicted count of 20, BSW lies within the comparator range. At predicted counts of 20 to 40, the robust variance of its locally standardized residuals is 2.7 times the binomial value, against 2.4 to 3.4 for the comparators. From a predicted count of 20, BSW recorded 20 precincts with three or fewer votes. The comparator rates at the same predicted counts give 5.5. The Poisson probability of 20 or more is $1.5 \times 10^{-6}$. Political variation specific to BSW would have to leave the body of the distribution and its moderate tail unaffected and act only at the extreme end. An adjacent minor party recorded at least 12 votes and ten times its predicted count in 16 precincts next to BSW, ten of them with three or fewer BSW votes. Across all comparator series this occurred seven times, never with the comparator at three or fewer (Fisher’s exact $p = 0.007$). [Precinct returns][wbz25] · [2017 precinct returns][wbz17] · [2021 precinct returns][wbz21]

**Subsidiary evidence from certification.**

- **Additional effort found additional errors.** After the first round of certification in North Rhine-Westphalia had corrected 819 BSW votes, all 64 district returning officers re-examined BSW results, using the BSW:BD ratio and other statistical deviations as triggers. The second pass found 476 more, 37% of the Land’s total.
- **Certification compares documents.** Bavaria’s Land returning officer states that counting, sorting, and protocol errors cannot be identified without recourse to the ballots. In the complainants’ compilation of the returning officers’ figures, recounts covered about 0.4% of precincts, 0.18% to 2.69% by Land.
- **Protocol errors survived certification.** In one Brandenburg precinct, 44 BSW votes recorded under BD in the signed protocol were found by the Land statistics office’s plausibility check after the district committee had certified.
- **Conspicuous returns were reported and left standing.** At least seven certified returns with extreme BSW deficits and BD surpluses were raised with the authorities and not recounted. One recorded 31 BD and zero BSW votes, while its two neighboring precincts recorded 31 and 41 BSW votes with one BD vote each. The protocol was checked and a correction refused.

[Bundestag 21/3100][bt3100]

**Forensic finding.** Reject the complete-removal hypothesis on the balance of the evidence. The exact test rejects local homogeneity. The inference of surviving administrative error rests on three additional observations taken together: the departures have the direction predicted by a transfer, the extreme group has no counterpart in the FDP controls, and ballot examinations during certification verified the same mechanism. Local political variation does not reproduce the signature. It moves first and second votes together, is indifferent to ballot order, and couples deficits and surpluses loosely. The BSW–BD extremes show the opposite on all three counts. The historical countertest extends the control from FDP to all mid-sized parties in three elections. None shows BSW’s excess at the extreme end or its destination-matched deficits. The comparators are exposed to the same undirected recording noise, so the excess is a lower bound for the BSW-specific component. This supports persistence of a conspicuous residual. Once the stack-like cases are removed, this test does not establish the prevalence or national magnitude of individual-vote swaps. [Documented certification corrections][bt3100]

### III. Materiality: What do the certified returns reveal about a diffuse residual?

**Null hypothesis.** Any net loss remaining after certification is too small to warrant further inquiry. A positive correction is consistent with this null: a residual confined to the visible survivors is the example at the low end.

**Causal alternative.** Certification preferentially detected conspicuous errors. Smaller net BSW losses could survive in precincts whose reported totals appeared ordinary. If those losses, together with the surviving conspicuous losses, outweigh corrections in the opposite direction after accounting for denominator changes, the expected BSW share after a full recount exceeds its certified share.

**Electoral consequence.** A positive expected correction and a correction sufficient to materially close in on 5% are separate claims. Let $\Delta B$ be the signed national BSW correction and $\Delta V$ the change in valid second votes. The electoral null is $\Delta B - 0.05\,\Delta V < 9{,}528.6$. The alternative is $\Delta B - 0.05\,\Delta V \ge 9{,}528.6$. The evidence can favor rejection of the equal-expected-share null while leaving both electoral outcomes plausible.

**Primary statistical comparison.** The surviving signed anomalies bear on the direction of the expected recount correction. To assess magnitude, the following comparison asks how well the certified distribution distinguishes negligible dispersed loss from dispersed loss on a scale that could affect representation. Set aside the ten conspicuous cases. Among 44,660 remaining precincts with comparable controls, 760 had a locally standardized BSW residual at or below −3. Simulations drew held-out FDP residuals, calibrated the background against the upper side of the BSW distribution, imposed specified net losses, and recomputed local expectations. The predictive score evaluates the full simulated distribution. Lower values indicate better predictions. [Precinct returns][wbz25]

| Additional net loss per precinct | Predicted median extreme count | 95% predictive interval | Predictive score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero | 737 | 651–828 | 14.8 |
| 0.10, dispersed small errors | 759 | 656–847 | 12.2 |
| 0.30, dispersed small errors | 795 | 691–903 | 21.1 |
| 0.30, concentrated in occasional nine-vote errors | 937 | 837–1,015 | — |

The intermediate dispersed scenario best predicts the observed 760 under the primary local specification. All three of the first intervals include 760. The constituency specification favored zero diffuse loss, while an uncalibrated FDP background favored 0.30. The stronger finding concerns the size of individual errors. A national mean loss of 0.30 concentrated in occasional nine-vote errors produced too many conspicuous precincts. The same national mean spread across small errors was much harder to distinguish from ordinary political variation.

**Subsidiary evidence from selected recounts.** In the 50 screened precincts, 39 were unchanged, nine gained 17 BSW votes in total, and two lost one each. The signed yield was +15, or +0.30 per checked precinct including zeros. Among the 11 changed precincts, nine or more gains have one-sided fair-sign probability 0.0327. Berlin contributed +5 across 40 precincts. The other ten contributed +10. This directly measures the examined sample and establishes that small adverse errors existed outside the specified conspicuous BSW screens. It supplies a scale for scenario construction. [Petition][petition] · [Bundestag 21/3100][bt3100]

**Visible allowance.** The scenarios assign 250 votes to the visible survivors. The certified returns support an estimate. North Rhine-Westphalia re-examined BSW results after its first round of certification, using the BSW:BD ratio as trigger (Section 8.II). A comparable trigger, BD at least 30% of BSW plus BD and at least five BD votes, was applied to the certified precincts of all Länder except Saarland. Flagged precincts carry 795 BD votes of excess over constituency-peer prediction, 84 of them in North Rhine-Westphalia. Counting only the excess above North Rhine-Westphalia’s post-review rate gives 451. Dividing by the matching slope of 0.81 gives approximately 550 to 1,000 BSW votes. In Hamburg, the trigger flags five precincts carrying 113 BD votes of excess, the maximum the Land returning officer stated. Stricter and looser triggers move the lower figure between 430 and 680. Independently, BSW’s excess of 14.5 extreme low counts over the comparators at equal predicted counts corresponds to roughly 350 votes, a lower bound for one part of this allowance. The diffuse residual remains assigned. [Precinct returns][wbz25] · [Bundestag 21/3100][bt3100]

**Forensic finding.** The extreme asymmetry in the certified returns, together with the signed corrections found in the screened recounts during certification, favors an upward expected national correction. On the forensic standard used here, that refutes equality of expected and certified shares. All three dispersed scenarios remain compatible with the observed count of 760 extreme precincts. The data therefore do not fully refute the third null of an immaterial residual. Nor can they settle on which side of 5% an accurate recount would fall.

## 9. Full findings and electoral consequence

### Endpoint models and intermediate scenario

| Model | Assumption | Implied national recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Conspicuous only (excluded by the persistence finding) | Virtually the entire surviving directional loss lies in the visible candidates. | About +250 |
| Partial susceptibility | A susceptible third of the remaining precincts averages +0.30 votes, including zeros, in addition to the estimated visible allowance of 550 to 1,000. | About +10,060 to +10,510 |
| Full susceptibility | The selected recount yield of +0.30 applies nationwide after certification. | About +28,500 |

The endpoints are estimates from two substantive models. They have no interpretation as statistical bounds. They fail in different ways. The conspicuous-only model fails in kind. Certification is suspicion-driven, so the more conspicuous an error, the more likely its discovery. The most conspicuous errors nonetheless survived (Section 8.II). Less conspicuous errors therefore survived at least at that rate. A model in which none survive reverses the detection ordering the inquiry establishes. Its votes belong only in the visible allowance. The full-susceptibility model fails in degree. It has the mechanism the evidence supports, diffuse errors surviving certification, but carries the certification-period yield of a selected sample forward undiminished to every precinct. The national residual therefore lies below +28,500 by a penalty applied to the susceptible fraction, to the mean net loss, or to both. The final precinct distribution formally excludes neither endpoint. The exclusion of the conspicuous-only model rests on the detection ordering.

The intermediate scenario has the strongest fit on the primary local diagnostic among the three rates examined. It also gives the assumed within-group rate of 0.30 an observed administrative reference. What the precinct distribution bears on is the product of the susceptible fraction $s$ and its mean net loss $\mu$. It cannot distinguish one-third susceptibility at 0.30 from one-half at 0.20. One-third is the working interpretation of an intermediate national mean of 0.10.

The intermediate scenario is best read as a tipping point. Starting from the full-susceptibility model, it applies the largest penalty at which the expected correction still reaches the threshold. Because the precinct distribution fixes only $s\mu$, the same point is reached by a susceptible third at 0.30, by about 0.09 in every precinct, or by any combination with that product. Expressed as a penalty, the expected correction reaches 5% unless the surviving diffuse rate lies more than about 70% below the yield found in the screened recounts. Further data or a sample recount can be measured directly against that penalty.

The table illustrates a common expected yield per precinct using the signed distribution from the 50 selected recounts. At +0.30 it retains the observed gain and loss frequencies. At +0.20 and +0.10 it applies two-thirds and one-third of those frequencies, respectively, holding the average sizes of gains and losses fixed. Other distributions can produce the same means. An unchanged net count can conceal offsetting mistakes.

| Expected net uplift per precinct | Lose votes | Unchanged | Gain votes | Net votes across 95,111 precincts | Relative to 5%, fixed denominator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +0.1 | 1.33% | 92.67% | 6.00% | +9,511 | 18 votes short |
| +0.2 | 2.67% | 85.33% | 12.00% | +19,022 | 9,494 votes above |
| +0.3 | 4.00% | 78.00% | 18.00% | +28,533 | 19,005 votes above |

At +0.10 across all 95,111 precincts, the entire expected recovery is +9,511. The +10,060 to +10,510 intermediate scenario instead credits the visible group with a separate allowance of 550 to 1,000 and applies the diffuse mean of approximately +0.10 only to the remaining precincts. That distinction explains why the two estimates fall on opposite sides of the fixed-denominator electoral threshold.

### Threshold arithmetic and probability

Let $\Delta B$ be the national BSW recount change and $\Delta V$ the change in valid second votes. Then

$$\frac{2{,}472{,}947 + \Delta B}{49{,}649{,}512 + \Delta V} \ge 0.05 \iff \Delta B - 0.05\,\Delta V \ge 9{,}528.6.$$

A transfer from another valid party contributes one threshold-equivalent vote. A newly recognized valid BSW ballot contributes 0.95. BSW needs 9,529 votes if all recoveries are valid-to-valid transfers and 10,031 if all are newly valid votes. [Certified national result][nat25]

With 550 to 1,000 votes recovered in the visible group, the break-even mean in a susceptible third of approximately 95,100 remaining precincts is

$$\frac{9{,}528.6 - 550}{95{,}100/3} = 0.283 \quad\text{to}\quad \frac{9{,}528.6 - 1{,}000}{95{,}100/3} = 0.269$$

threshold-equivalent votes. The observed certification-period yield in the selected recounts was 0.30 BSW votes. Applying that yield to the susceptible third gives about +10,060 to +10,510, approximately 530 to 980 votes above the fixed-denominator threshold. Transferring the rate from the selected certification sample to surviving errors is the substantive extrapolation. On the further assumption that all +4,277 certified gains were recovery of the initial directional loss, a threshold-sized residual would mean certification recovered only about 31% of that loss.

| Probability specification | Probability of reaching 5% |
|---|---|
| Undirected Gaussian background, independent precincts, five-vote precinct standard deviation | $3.2 \times 10^{-10}$ |
| Same background with fourfold aggregate variance | 0.1% |
| Directional headline model | 70%–72% |
| Same directional model with newly valid diffuse recoveries | 66%–69% |
| Same endpoints and mode, beta-PERT distribution | 62%–65% |
| Visible allowance of 250 instead of the estimate | 68% |

The visible-only case is excluded by the persistence finding and receives no weight. The headline model uses a triangular distribution with minimum at the visible allowance, mode at the intermediate scenario, and maximum 28,500. With the allowance at 550 to 1,000, its fixed-denominator crossing probability is approximately 70% to 72%. The differences between specifications have little bearing on the investigation. The material distinction is between a remote crossing under the specified undirected-error model and a substantial crossing probability under a dispersed directional process.

## 10. Proposed verification in order of evidentiary value and effort

The following proposes potential “minimally invasive” undertakings to discriminate between the proposed hypotheses at a far lower effort and disruption than a full recount. With comparatively limited resource deployment, a clear picture about the potential material effect of a recount can be discerned before such a recount is offered.

**First, publish the precinct-level preliminary returns.** Pair each preliminary return with its certified counterpart and calculate the signed changes in BSW, BD, other parties, valid second votes, and invalid second votes. Three population statistics would materially advance the inquiry without reopening ballots:

1. **The distribution of certification corrections by size and sign.** Separate one- to three-vote changes from larger corrections. Measure gross gains, gross losses, and net recovery in each band. This establishes whether a few large events or many small ones generated the +4,277.
2. **The joint distribution of category changes.** Measure how often BSW gains coincide with compensating BD losses, changes in invalid votes, or changes elsewhere. Stratify by Land, precinct type, and size. This tests the specific BD prediction while retaining the all-cause national estimand.
3. **Recovery as a function of preliminary conspicuousness.** Define anomaly strength from the preliminary returns, then measure subsequent correction rates and yields across the full range. This directly tests whether consolidation concentrated on conspicuous precincts and whether directional gains were also recovered from ordinary-looking ones. The share of conspicuous preliminary anomalies that certification corrected, $d$, is an upper limit on the detection rate for less conspicuous errors. The surviving loss from less conspicuous errors is then at least their recovered gain × $(1 - d)/d$.

Paired returns can establish what certification changed. An error appearing identically in preliminary and certified returns produces a zero change. Publication could therefore sharpen the residual estimate and narrow the recount required without, by itself, revealing every surviving error.

**Second, if the electoral question remains unresolved, conduct a limited recount.** Recount the strongest flagged precincts and record their verified threshold-equivalent contribution $A$. Independently draw a stratified probability sample from the remainder. Record every signed change, including losses and zeros. For sample inclusion probabilities $\pi_i$, estimate the national threshold-equivalent correction by

$$\hat{T} = A + \sum_{i \in S} \frac{\Delta B_i - 0.05\,\Delta V_i}{\pi_i}.$$

If the targeted group yields the estimated 550 to 1,000 votes, the decisive comparison in the rest of the country is 0.090 to 0.094 threshold-equivalent votes per precinct. The random probe can estimate that signed mean directly. It does not require identifying susceptible precincts in advance or reconstructing individual gross flows that canceled. The same estimate and its uncertainty can be assessed against any proposed lower magnitude.

Stratify the sample by the certification examination log, separating precincts examined during certification from those never examined. In the latter, the residual equals the original error. Draw precincts with probability proportional to preliminary BSW votes, since the expected loss scales with them. With the dispersion observed in the 50 screened recounts, 0.78 to 1.32 votes per precinct, placing the decisive mean two standard errors from the full-susceptibility rate of 0.30 requires 60 to 170 precincts; from zero, 250 to 730; from half the decisive mean, 1,000 to 2,900.
